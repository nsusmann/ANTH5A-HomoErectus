Bibliographic Sources

Peer-reviewed papers and reviews relevant to the Homo erectus/Homo ergaster comparisons in the map. The papers represent competing taxonomic interpretations; they do not establish one universally accepted list of H. ergaster fossils.

How to read these sources: some papers use H. ergaster for early African fossils, some include those fossils in H. erectus sensu lato, and individual specimens such as SK 847 and the Dmanisi fossils have competing assignments. A marker in one model records that model’s choice, not a settled identification.

Taxonomic frameworks and broad comparisons

  1. Wood, B., & Lonergan, N. (2008). The hominin fossil record: taxa, grades and clades. Journal of Anatomy, 212(4), 354–376. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-7580.2008.00871.x. Recognizes H. ergaster, names KNM-ER 992 as its type specimen, and lists Koobi Fora and West Turkana as core evidence, with Dmanisi as possible evidence.
  2. Antón, S. C. (2003). Natural history of Homo erectus. American Journal of Physical Anthropology, 122(S37), 126–170. https://doi.org/10.1002/ajpa.10399. Reviews the geographic and morphological variation and taxonomic debate; catalogues early African material under broad H. erectus sensu lato, with SK 847 marked uncertain.
  3. Antón, S. C., & Middleton, E. R. (2023). Making meaning from fragmentary fossils: Early Homo in the Early to early Middle Pleistocene. Journal of Human Evolution, 179, 103307. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhevol.2022.103307. Recent review of early Homo taxonomy, dispersal, and classification issues.

Specimen and locality studies

  1. Brown, F., Harris, J., Leakey, R., & Walker, A. (1985). Early Homo erectus skeleton from west Lake Turkana, Kenya. Nature, 316(6031), 788–792. https://doi.org/10.1038/316788a0. Original report of KNM-WT 15000, described as H. erectus.
  2. Gabunia, L., Vekua, A., Lordkipanidze, D., et al. (2000). Earliest Pleistocene hominid cranial remains from Dmanisi, Republic of Georgia: Taxonomy, geological setting, and age. Science, 288(5468), 1019–1025. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.288.5468.1019. Compares Dmanisi crania with H. ergaster from Koobi Fora; the authors note the mandible’s taxonomic affinity is uncertain.
  3. Rosas, A., & Bermúdez de Castro, J. M. (1998). On the taxonomic affinities of the Dmanisi mandible (Georgia). American Journal of Physical Anthropology, 107(2), 145–162. PubMed record. Proposes the tentative identification Homo sp. indet. (aff. ergaster) for the Dmanisi mandible.
  4. Lordkipanidze, D., Ponce de León, M. S., Margvelashvili, A., et al. (2013). A complete skull from Dmanisi, Georgia, and the evolutionary biology of early Homo. Science, 342(6156), 326–331. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1238484. Interprets the Dmanisi crania as a single, highly variable regional population of H. erectus.
  5. Spoor, F., Leakey, M. G., Gathogo, P. N., et al. (2007). Implications of new early Homo fossils from Ileret, east of Lake Turkana, Kenya. Nature, 448, 688–691. https://doi.org/10.1038/nature05986. Describes KNM-ER 42700 as an African H. erectus fossil; placing it under H. ergaster depends on the split model used.
  6. Grine, F. E., Demes, B., Jungers, W. L., & Cole, T. M. (1993). Taxonomic affinity of the early Homo cranium from Swartkrans, South Africa. American Journal of Physical Anthropology, 92(4), 411–426. https://doi.org/10.1002/ajpa.1330920402. Finds SK 847’s attribution to early H. erectus unlikely and suggests closest affinity with H. habilis sensu lato or another early Homo.
  7. Bilsborough, A. (2002). Craniofacial diversity in early Homo and the affinities of the Sterkfontein Valley specimens. Human Evolution, 17(1–2), 21–32. https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02436426. Notes some resemblance between SK 847 and early African H. erectus (= H. ergaster), but argues its distinct facial features militate against inclusion in that species.
  8. Berger, L. R., et al. (2015). Homo naledi, a new species of the genus Homo from the Dinaledi Chamber, South Africa. eLife, 4, e09560. https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.09560. In its comparative framework, treats “H. ergaster” as a synonym of H. erectus and includes KNM-ER 992, 1808, 3733, 3883, 42700, KNM-WT 15000, and SK 847 in its H. erectus sample.

Foundational species proposal

  1. Groves, C. P., & Mazák, V. (1975). An approach to the taxonomy of the Hominidae: Gracile Villafranchian hominids of Africa. Časopis pro Mineralogii a Geologii, 20(3), 225–247. The original proposal of Homo ergaster, based on KNM-ER 992 and other East African material.
